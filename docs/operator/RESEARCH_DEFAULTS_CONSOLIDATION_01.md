## A. Decision and its limits

The promoted configuration is **GPT-6.1 Terra/medium for every Research decision,
GPT-6.1 Sol/medium for Answer, Lite for the first eligible generic Exa search of
a fresh session, and Auto for subsequent generic searches**. Six results,
Dynamic/high query-guided Highlights, actual source text, acquisition limits,
Evidence admission and citation custody remain unchanged. A retained-session
follow-up continues to use Auto. Service-tier configuration and transport behavior
are unchanged.

Research retains its existing optional, nullable and revisable briefing. A null
update preserves the latest account; newly established findings can revise it.
The briefing is turn-local interpretation, never Evidence, citation authority or
durable session state. Answer retains final judgment and can inspect originals
or request delegated Research before resuming the same Answer interaction.

This is a **practical, reversible product choice**, not a finding of generally
established Terra superiority. The allocation campaign's strongest DOE answer
came from all-Terra, and its four-question descriptive total was lower than
all-Luna's. However, opening decisions, searches and acquired evidence differed.
The identical-evidence continuation campaign subsequently showed competent Luna
continuation and no positive mandatory-direction effect. Neither campaign solved
DOE eligibility completeness or Klamath coverage.

The authorized all-Terra choice keeps one Research configuration for initial,
continued and delegated decisions. A hybrid would add model-selection policy
without a demonstrated quality or personal-plan billing benefit from that extra
allocation policy. Its
first campaign never supplied a non-null opening briefing; the later campaign
demonstrated continuity but no correct initial DOE framing to transfer. Simplicity
and the observed all-Terra DOE improvement support this bounded choice; they do
not establish a universal ranking of Terra and Luna.

Lite-first has the clearer practical retrieval case: seven completed product
comparisons met Lite's registered answer obligations, six were faster, and the
median paired saving was approximately 6.6 seconds. Passport required recovery
and was slower. Deep still found valuable specialist material missed by faster
packets. Lite-first therefore carries a documented coverage risk rather than a
universal quality or latency guarantee. No automatic Deep fallback, LinkUp pairing,
dynamic allocation, planner, Analyst, reviewer, mandatory initial direction or
briefing persistence is promoted. Prompts and schemas are unchanged.

### 1. Discovery: content candidates are not completed answers

S1 used exactly frozen queries, concurrent Deep/Lite/LinkUp requests within each
case, sequential cases, and no retries or endpoint failures. D6/L6/F6 denote six
valid requested results. C6 chose three Lite then three unique LinkUp results,
with same-lane duplicate refill; C12 was the deduplicated full fast union. Each
page's returned text remained separate. The experiment did not merge passages
from different sources or send these packets to a product model.

Median retrieval times were **Deep 8.408s, Lite 0.721s, LinkUp Fast 1.085s and the
concurrent fast pair 1.115s**. The pair was faster in 50/50, median paired saving
7.289s. These are not whole-turn metrics: Deep's p95 was 13.948s,
the pair's 1.507s. Deep returned 295 valid URLs, Lite and LinkUp 300 each. Lite
returned six in every case; no specific Lite cache omission was identified.

The semantic evaluation counted 161 frozen obligations. Sufficient-looking
source-attributed content candidates covered D6 143, L6 144, F6 130, C6/C12 147.
Complete candidate cases were **36/50, 37/50, 27/50, 40/50 and 41/50** respectively.
These are discovery/completeness judgments, not verified truth, complete context,
or observed Answer quality. C6 had eight complete cases absent from D6, while
D6 had four absent from C6; even C12 missed three complete Deep cases. Fixed
3+3 also displaced useful Lite sources in a refund-rule case. Under a 12,000
character cap C6 fell to 39 complete candidates. Equal result counts did not
hold source volume or model attention equal.

Complementary material included the October 2025 Klamath tributary milestone,
actual Artemis launch chronology, BYD's BEV/PHEV denominator, labor swearing-in
chronology, community discussion and aviation-disruption context. Deep's
specialist advantages included an MD-80 cost-model lead, original manufacturer
aircraft specifications, final NTSB Dali findings and 2023 Nevada/Mexico water
allocation amounts. Several packets remained insufficient for their questions
even where one provider supplied a real advantage.

The bounded fidelity check inspected ten predetermined LinkUp excerpts, one Lite
and one Deep, with exactly twelve credential-free original GET attempts. Of ten
LinkUp results, one matched a substantive source passage, one was a faithful
condensation, two joined/reordered or lost context, one had an apparent
claim/provenance mismatch in the inspected visible original, and five originals
were inaccessible or otherwise unverified. Both sampled Exa spans matched after
layout normalization. Neither small sample establishes a general fidelity rate.
Inaccessible originals were not labeled false; correspondence to a source did
not independently verify its assertions.

LinkUp text could contain useful attributable facts, but could not automatically
be called a contiguous verified passage. Missing applicability, dates, exceptions
or denominators still required acquisition. Pairing was not adopted because its
modest extra discovery coverage had no demonstrated completed-product advantage,
added transport/admission complexity, and left these context/provenance concerns.
The later product evidence supported the simpler Lite-only choice.

### 4. Organic DOE: the neighboring question was answered well

The original user question contained **"skill of the worker"**. Before receiving
sources, Research considered job/skill grading and safety/exposure classification,
then reframed the investigation around general worker competence. Its searches
replaced the phrase with competence, training, qualification and authorization.
The explicit DOE national-laboratory follow-up did not correct that direction.
The original phrase remained available in conversation; Research's turn-2
briefing began null, so no durable briefing secretly carried the prior frame.

The organic Reading Room's actual modes were **Lite/Auto on turn 1 and Auto/Auto
on turn 2**. This was not a controlled retrieval comparison. **The DOE follow-up
failure must not be attributed to Lite.** Query-directed Highlights plausibly
reinforced the chosen interpretation, but retrieval causality was not isolated.

The relevant DOE-HDBK-1211-2014 URL was discovered as E14. Its 2,845-character
highlight ended inside the SOW paragraph, after the words "based upon". The
named heading, remainder of the paragraph, handbook's own SOW Appendix A pointer
and actual Appendix A activity-eligibility criteria were absent. Other Appendix A
strings in order citations were not that pointer. The incomplete provider-returned
boundary preceded model exposure; exact stored/exposed text matched. There is
no evidence of later attention-packet trimming or of an acquired Appendix being
discarded at selection.

Research did not pursue the visible unfinished SOW lead. It selected E14 intact
with worker-authorization sources and briefed qualifications, KSAs, supervision,
OJT and order applicability. Sol received that evidence and produced a
substantially supported answer about the neighboring question. It did not explain
**eligibility of an activity for SOW's limited-planning treatment**, which is
different from **qualification or authorization of a worker**. Valid citations
and a runtime `supported` posture did not establish task completeness.

The handbook is nonmandatory work-planning/control guidance with contractor,
facility and site applicability limits, not a universal compulsory DOE worker
grade. This contextual distinction survives in the adjudication; the ambiguous
original wording is not retroactively turned into an unambiguous prompt.

## C. Unresolved Research-competence frontier

**Changing Research to Terra and the first search to Lite does not solve the
problems that motivated these investigations.** Concrete surviving capabilities
include:

- Recognizing a technical term of art when ordinary wording has another plausible
  meaning: DOE's “skill of the worker” can name an activity work-control category,
  rather than a worker's general competence or job grade.
- Revising initial direction when domain evidence warrants it: a DOE refinement,
  incomplete SOW paragraph or visible handbook pointer did not reliably redirect
  the investigation toward controlling criteria.
- Following incomplete excerpts to originals: finding the handbook URL or reading
  the same retained highlight did not acquire Appendix A. A full parent and
  focused navigation were mechanically available, but autonomous pursuit remained
  undemonstrated.
- Recognizing partial criteria: stable/preassessed hazards establish part of SOW,
  not all exclusions involving permits, facility/safety basis, configuration,
  critical instructions and independent verification.
- Acquiring consequential missing chronology and scope: allocation/shared-direction
  Klamath runs missed October 2025 construction completion on Scotch, Camp, Jenny
  and Beaver creeks and Spencer Creek's separate restoration scope. Fish observations
  at Spencer do not supply that construction evidence.
- Carrying eligibility exclusions and qualifications through selection and Answer:
  DOE's received permit exclusion disappeared in the genuine-briefing continuation
  selections, while the omission controls retained it. NTSB's bridge contributor
  also disappeared at different layers in different observations.
- Making additional Research worthwhile: some Klamath delegation resolved real
  ambiguity, while sparse-employment and unused lexical searches repeated low-yield
  work. Neither “always search more” nor a mandatory early-stop rule is established.

The failure layers must remain distinct:

| Layer | Concrete observation | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wrong initial interpretation | Organic DOE searches replaced the term with worker-competence language before sources returned. | That Lite caused the follow-up failure. |
| Missing acquisition | Organic DOE never acquired Appendix A; allocation/direction Klamath never acquired the October milestone/Spencer scope. | That Answer ignored criteria it never received. |
| Failure to pursue an available lead | Completed-paragraph R4 exposed the genuine Appendix pointer but Luna returned without pursuing it; later Terra DOE also left a pointer unresolved. | That the organic truncated excerpt already contained that same pointer. |
| Useful evidence acquired but not delivered | Shared-direction DOE had the safety-permit exclusion in common originals but omitted its source from the genuine-briefing Answer selections. | That the source provider failed to discover it. |
| Evidence/account delivered but not used | Final Baltimore Deep had the contributor in its briefing but final Answer omitted it, while its exact source excerpt was incomplete. | A clean test of ignoring fully received complete source support. |

These are different problems. “The search failed” conceals whether correction
requires interpretation, acquisition, pursuit, selection or faithful final use.

## D. Rejected or unproven approaches

**Reordering context:** two completed user-first probes did not recover the DOE
category and both returned prematurely. This specific intervention earned no
production correction. The public-packet reconstruction and remaining source
context limit general claims about conversational anchoring.

**Removing previous assistant prose:** two completed ablations still pursued
generic competence. Prior prose alone was not a sufficient explanation or remedy
in those packets. This does not prove conversation can never anchor interpretation.
